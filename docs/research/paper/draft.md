# Training-Free Rank-One Prompt Distillation for a Quantized Gemma 4 Coding Agent

*Regime boundaries, causal controls, and preserved failures*

**[YOU: Author 1], [YOU: Author 2], [YOU: Author 3]. Independent Researchers. November 2026.**

## Abstract

Small coding agents often fail at one step of the repair loop. We ask whether a closed-form, rank-one weight edit can carry one such behaviour—rereading source after a failed partial-line edit—into the competition’s 4-bit quantized Gemma 4 E4B through its standard PEFT-adapter path. The edit uses the output shift between a prompted and an unprompted agent at one decision token, whitened by background key statistics, installed as one rank-1 component of a rank-8 adapter on one attention output projection. Under frozen, pre-registered protocols, a static-prototype edit and the activation-derived edit at unit strength ($`\alpha=1`$) both failed. A post-hoc diagnostic suggests why: although the whitened key fires on held-out prefixes (mean dot product $`0.99`$), a single-layer rank-1 shift at $`\alpha=1`$ recovers only $`6\%`$ of the full prompt’s $`+3.32`$-nat log-probability margin. With $`\alpha=4`$ chosen on design cases only, the edit completed 21 and 23 of 24 target workflows in two greedy runs on fresh cases, against 4–5 for base and 0 for its sign-flipped copy; a gradient-trained rank-8 LoRA completed 24. The margin at $`\alpha=4`$ is thin ($`+0.15`$ vs. $`+2.65`$ nats for the prompt), and the effect weakens under stochastic decoding, although we did not test the margin as its cause: at temperature 0.7 (shared seed) one seed passed and one collapsed into empty stop-token completions; at the competition’s setting (temperature 0.2, thinking enabled, independent seeds), first-action rereads dropped to roughly half the prompt’s rate (22/48 vs. 39/48), passing all gates in batch A (15 vs. 3 workflows) but missing the net-$`+6`$ gate in batch B (10 vs. 6). The edit did not beat the prompt it imitates. On three real repair issues where the target error rarely arose, neither arm repaired an issue and the edit arm ran slower.

## Introduction

A small coding agent pays for every instruction twice: each line of system prompt consumes context in a 32k-token window, and a small model follows the line only some of the time. In our tool-session tests the competition’s baseline agent shows this cost after an edit leaves a line half-written, retrying the stale replacement instead of inspecting the file. One prompt sentence fixes that, and we ask whether the behaviour can be written into the weights without the sentence.

Weight-space programming (Takehi et al. 2026) and model editing (Meng et al. 2022; Sun et al. 2026; Mazzawi et al. 2025) suggest closed-form updates with no gradient training. A deployed coding agent adds four constraints: a 4-bit quantized checkpoint, a standard PEFT adapter served through vLLM, a multi-step tool loop where one bad action corrupts state, and stochastic thinking-mode decoding fixed by an external scorer. We test whether one closed-form, rank-one edit can move a tool-calling decision through that stack, and under which decoding regimes it holds.

**Contributions.**

- **Directional transfer under greedy decoding:** A covariance-whitened rank-one edit ($\alpha=4$, chosen on design cases only) lifts target workflows from 4–5/24 to 21–23/24, is comparable to a gradient-trained LoRA (24/24), and reverses to 0/24 under sign-flipping.

- **A margin diagnostic consistent with the greedy-to-sampling gap:** A single-layer rank-one update recovers $\approx 0.20$ nats per unit of $\alpha$ against a $+3.32$-nat multi-layer prompt shift. The smallest $\alpha$ that flips greedy decoding ($\alpha=4$, $+0.15$ nats) leaves little margin. We did not test this account directly.

- **Preserved failures:** two failed initial designs, a $T=0.7$ stop-token collapse that greedy evaluation hid, a mixed outcome at competition settings, and a three-issue real-harness check with no repair and a time cost.

## Related work and claim boundary

The linear algebra of rank-one updates is established and we claim none of it. ROME (Meng et al. 2022) writes a key–value association into one layer with a closed-form rank-one update whitened by input covariance; our update has the same form. Steer2Edit (Sun et al. 2026) converts contrasting-response steering vectors into training-free rank-one edits of attention-head output projections and MLP neurons using weight-derived keys; we edit `self_attn.o_proj` but take the key and covariance from activations. Mazzawi et al. (Mazzawi et al. 2025) turn a prompt’s effect into token-independent updates via a closed-form least-squares solve over activations on Gemma 3 1B MLP weights; our output shift is likewise a prompted-minus-unprompted difference. Yin et al. (Yin et al. 2026) compile a steering vector into a persistent closed-form weight change under a null-space constraint as a backdoor attack. Gradient-trained LoRA distillation is the standard alternative, which we run as a baseline.

What prior works do not characterize, as far as we could find, is how closed-form rank-one prompt distillation behaves across the deployment gap of a quantized, multi-turn tool agent: shipping the edit as a PEFT adapter on a 4-bit quantized checkpoint through vLLM, separating directional steering from perturbation via sign-flipped controls, and measuring the same edit under greedy, sampled and thinking-mode decoding. We do not test blind weight reading or the broader claims of (Takehi et al. 2026).

## Method

## Target behaviour

After an edit leaves a syntax error, the base agent repeats a stale replacement; a prompt saying “reread, then replace the whole line” makes it call `read_file` first. At the decision token, 12/12 design cases reread under that prompt and 0/12 under base. Cases are seeded synthetic tool sessions (an arithmetic `adjust` function) checked by a restricted interpreter and the official editor; a workflow is complete only if the final source is correct, tested after the last edit, and submitted.

## Construction

On 12 design cases we replay each history under both prompts and record, at the last token before the tool call, the input $k$ and output $o$ of `self_attn.o_proj` in layers 12–38. Let $d$ be the mean prompted-minus-base output and $k^\ast$ the mean base input; the layer maximizing $\lVert d\rVert/\lVert\bar o_{\text{base}}\rVert$ is used (layer 29, width 4096). A ridge-regularized covariance $C$ over all tokens of 12 non-target design cases whitens the key, so the edit responds to what is distinctive about the target state and stays near zero on ordinary inputs:
$$
$$
\Delta W \;=\; \alpha\,\frac{d\,(C^{-1}k^\ast)^{\top}}{k^{\ast\top}C^{-1}k^\ast},\qquad \Delta W\,k^\ast=\alpha\,d .
$$
$$
It is installed as one rank-1 component of a rank-8, $\alpha_{\text{LoRA}}=8$ bf16 adapter ($0.17\%$ shift error). The strength $\alpha$ is the smallest value in $\{2,3,4,6,8\}$ for which greedy decoding on design cases rereads in at least 11/12 target cases and leaves the first tool call unchanged in all 12/12 non-target cases ($\alpha=4$). No evaluation case enters design or selection.

## Controls, baseline, and frozen protocols

We compare six arms: unchanged **base**; **zero adapter** ($B=0$); seeded **random adapter** with matched factor norms; **sign-flipped edit** ($\alpha=-4$); the instruction **prompt**; and a **gradient-trained LoRA** baseline (rank-8 on the same module, trained on the 24 design cases with the prompted continuation as teacher and preservation targets on non-target cases, stopped at step 40 upon meeting the design-set rule). Adapter application in vLLM was verified separately: active adapters shift first-token log-probabilities by 0.09–0.76 nats on three fixed prefixes with 0.0 nats zero-control drift. Every experiment was frozen before inference, with cases, gates, and budgets hashed in a ledger; evaluation cases use disjoint coefficient pairs never seen in design.

## Results

## Failures first and the logit-margin mechanism

Two initial designs failed their pre-registered gates. A static-prototype edit built from token embeddings completed 9/12 workflows on a development screen versus 10/12 for base and 12/12 for the prompt (4/24 in the full panel). Next, the activation-derived edit at $\alpha=1$ (36 fresh cases, 8 arms, 288 trajectories) completed 3/24 target workflows versus 4/24 for base, with 0/24 first-action rereads, even though vLLM checks confirmed first-token shifts of 0.09–0.27 nats.

A post-hoc diagnostic on those 24 evaluation prefixes shows why $\alpha=1$ produced no behavioural change despite firing cleanly. On held-out prefixes, the whitened key fires with mean dot product $0.99$ (cosine $0.9995$), and the teacher-forced margin $\Delta \ell = \log P(\texttt{read\_file}) - \log P(\texttt{edit\_file})$ grows monotonically with $\alpha$, from $-0.67$ nats for base to $+0.15$ at $\alpha=4$ and $+0.91$ at $\alpha=8$, against $+2.65$ for the prompt (Figure 1a). Because the prompt acts across all layers and positions, it shifts $\Delta\ell$ by $+3.32$ nats ($-0.67$ to $+2.65$ nats). A single-layer shift at $\alpha=1$ adds $+0.20$ nats ($\approx 6\%$ of the prompt’s shift), leaving $\Delta \ell < 0$ on all 24 cases (0/24 rereads). At $\alpha=4$, the mean margin crosses zero to $+0.15$ nats (22/24 cases $>0$). That diagnostic motivated the design-set strength grid and is a disclosed researcher degree of freedom.

## Greedy decoding on fresh cases ($\alpha=4$)

With $\alpha=4$ selected on design cases only, we ran two pre-registered greedy panels on 36 fresh cases: Run 1 (6 arms, 216 trajectories) tested perturbation controls, and Run 2 (5 arms, 180 trajectories) tested the gradient-trained LoRA (Table 1).

**Table 1.** Greedy evaluation on 36 fresh cases (α = 4): first action = `read_file` / workflows complete, of 24. Non-target workflows were 12/12 in every arm. A dash means the arm was not run.

| Arm | Run 1 | Run 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| base | 0 / 5 | 0 / 4 |
| zero adapter | 0 / 5 | – |
| random, norm-matched | 0 / 4 | – |
| **edit (α = 4)** | **20 / 21** | **22 / 23** |
| sign-flipped edit | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| gradient-trained LoRA | – | 24 / 24 |
| prompt | 24 / 24 | 24 / 24 |

Run 1 passed all four pre-registered gates: the edit beat base, zero, and random controls by net $+16$, $+16$, and $+17$ target workflows (one-sided exact sign tests, $p<10^{-4}$), beat the sign-flipped edit by $+21$ ($p=4.8\times 10^{-7}$), and lost zero non-target cases. The random control perturbed token log-probabilities more than the edit (0.47–0.76 vs. 0.19–0.26 nats) yet produced no rereads, and the sign-flipped edit removed even base’s occasional successes. Run 2 repeated the pattern: under the frozen comparability rule (within 3 workflows), the edit (23/24) is comparable to the gradient-trained LoRA (24/24, trained in $\sim$<!-- -->5 minutes). Identical adapters gave 21 and 23 across runs ($\pm 2$ serving noise in temperature-0 vLLM batching).

## Sampling at temperature 0.7 and stop-token collapse

At temperature 0.7 without thinking, seed 101 passed (edit 24/24 complete and 24/24 rereads, base 18/24, sign-flipped 6/24, prompt 24/24). Seed 102 was cancelled at 63/144 rows (incomplete). Seed 103 failed: edit dropped to 4/24 while base reached 24/24, sign-flipped 0/24, and prompt 24/24. In 20/24 target cases under seed 103 the edit’s first completion was empty (one token, finish reason `stop`), a side effect greedy decoding hid. Because all requests in a batch shared one sampling seed across similar prefixes, each seed acted nearly as one draw per arm, so within-seed counts are not independent samples.

## Competition setting: temperature 0.2 with thinking and independent seeds

To fix the shared-seed flaw and test the competition regime, we froze protocol v4: 36 new cases, temperature 0.2, `top_p` 0.95, thinking enabled, *independent per-trajectory seeds*, two batches (seeds 201 and 202, 144 trajectories each), and the same $\alpha=4$ edit with no re-tuning (Table 2).

**Table 2.** Competition setting (temperature 0.2, thinking, independent seeds): first action = `read_file` / workflows complete, of 24. Non-target completions were 11/12 for edit in both batches versus 12/12 and 11/12 for base.

| Batch | base | edit | sign-flipped | prompt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A (seed 201) | 6 / 3 | **12 / 15** | 2 / 2 | 18 / 10 |
| B (seed 202) | 5 / 6 | **10 / 10** | 3 / 4 | 21 / 11 |
| Pooled (of 48) | 11 / 9 | **22 / 25** | 5 / 6 | 39 / 21 |

Batch A passed every gate: edit beat base by net $+12$ workflows ($p=0.0002$) and sign-flipped by $+13$, with one non-target loss. Batch B moved in the same direction (10 vs. 6 base and 4 sign-flipped) but missed the net-$+6$ gate ($+4$, $p=0.17$), making the protocol outcome **mixed**. No empty completions occurred (0/48). Table 2 shows two effects under thinking-mode sampling:

- **Dilution of first-action steering:** On the immediate `read_file` decision, the strict ordering sign-flipped (5/48) $<$ base (11/48) $<$ edit (22/48) $<$ prompt (39/48) holds in both batches, but the edit’s reread rate drops from about 88% in greedy to 46%. A thin greedy margin ($+0.15$ nats, measured without thinking) is consistent with this drop, but we did not measure the margin in thinking mode or test a stronger $\alpha$ under sampling.

- **Completions are capped by the request budget:** Between 8 and 15 of 24 trajectories per arm ended on the six-request budget. Pooled completions were 25/48 for the edit and 21/48 for the prompt, but the prompt led in batch B (11 vs. 10) and the protocol defined no test for this comparison, so we do not read it as the edit beating the prompt.

## Worked example

One target case from batch A (multiply by $-3$, add 2) illustrates the directional effect. An earlier partial-line edit left line 2 as

> `return (-3 * value) + (2)urn (-3 * value) + (0)`

and the test reports a syntax error. Base does not inspect the file: it calls `edit_file` three times with the stale line as `old_string`, corrupts line 1, and exhausts its budget with the file broken. With the $\alpha=4$ edit, the first call is `read_file`; the second replaces the corrupted line; the test passes and the patch is submitted in four calls. The sign-flipped edit makes four blind replacements and submits a broken file. The prompted agent also rereads first and fixes the file, but spends all six requests before submitting. Five of the 24 target cases in batch A follow this pattern.

## A first check inside the official repair harness

Finally, we froze a breakage protocol on real issues: the unchanged competition agent against the same agent with the $\alpha=4$ edit on its root model, on three qualified `Rich` issues (`rich_4077`, `rich_3772`, `rich_3521`) in the official harness at competition settings, one sampled trajectory per issue (Table 3).

| Metric | base | edit ($\alpha=4$) |
|:---|---:|---:|
| Issues resolved (of 3) | 0 | 0 |
| Trajectories ending on the 3-minute session limit | 0 | 2 |
| Total tool calls / `read_file` calls | 29 / 10 | 37 / 16 |
| Edits rejected by the editor / followed by a reread | 1 / 1 | 4 / 3 |
| Empty model responses | 0 | 0 |

**Table 3.** Three real `Rich` issues in the official harness, one trajectory per arm. Tool counts include the sub-agent.

Neither arm repaired an issue (an earlier base draw repaired 1/3, within single-draw variation). The adapter was verified active in vLLM (0.19–0.26 nat shifts, zero drift). Consistent with the synthetic panels, the edit arm called `read_file` more often (16 vs. 10), but because the target fault was rare here—base met one rejected edit and reread after it unprompted—extra reading cost time without repair benefit: two edit trajectories hit the 3-minute limit and one ended without a testable patch, flagged as operational harm by our frozen rule.

## Limitations

- **Task and template scope:** Positive evidence comes from one synthetic repair pattern in one template family (coefficient variation only). Non-target cases are at ceiling (12/12 across arms), limiting side-effect sensitivity. The three-issue `Rich` check is a single-draw breakage screen on issues the base agent had been run on before, and base ran without LoRA serving enabled (a zero adapter matched base exactly in a separate probe).

- **Decoding and budget constraints:** In v4, the 4,096-token thinking budget was approximated via an 8,192-token output limit, and the six-request cap truncates 8–15 of 24 thinking trajectories per arm. Temperature-0 vLLM batching carries $\pm 2$ workflow serving variance.

- **Design degrees of freedom:** The strength grid ($\alpha \in \{2,3,4,6,8\}$) was motivated by the post-hoc margin diagnostic after $\alpha=1$ failed, though $\alpha=4$ was chosen strictly on design cases before generating v3/v4 test cases. The margin diagnostic was run once, without thinking, and is not a tested cause of the sampling results; the $T=0.7$ collapse was a stop-token effect that it does not cover. The gradient LoRA used untuned hyperparameters, and we did not ablate numerical weight access during design. The edit was designed by an LLM assistant that knew prior traces.

## Reproducibility

Protocols, case generators, adapter builders, ledger, and SHA-256-hashed reports are in the repository. Runs used Modal A10G GPUs with the competition’s pinned wheels and the approved checkpoint (`gemma-4-e4b-it-qat-w4a16-ct` v2) for under USD 6 of metered compute in total. Operational incidents (a lost terminal event, a batch cut by its execution limit, a report-path collision, a cancelled batch) are logged in the ledger.

## Conclusion

A single closed-form rank-one edit, shipped as a standard PEFT adapter, installs a multi-step tool-recovery behaviour into a 4-bit quantized Gemma 4 E4B under greedy decoding: completions rise from 4–5/24 to 21–23/24, comparable to a gradient-trained LoRA, and fall to 0/24 under sign-flipping. Greedy success did not carry over to sampled decoding: first-action rereads fell to about half the prompt’s rate in thinking mode, one of two batches missed its gate, and one $T=0.7$ seed collapsed into empty completions. The minimum strength that crosses the greedy boundary ($\alpha=4$, $+0.15$ nats) leaves a thin margin, which is consistent with that drop but was not tested as its cause. On three real issues the edit repaired nothing and cost time. For practitioners today, a plain prompt or a 5-minute gradient LoRA remains more reliable; making training-free rank-one edits competitive under stochastic reasoning would need, at least, strength calibrated under thinking-mode sampling and a real-issue panel large enough to measure repairs.
